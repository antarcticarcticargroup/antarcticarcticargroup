The **Antarctic & Arctic Atmospheric Rivers Group (AAARG!)** formed in 2022 with the first dedicated workshop, initially under the name _Antarctic Atmospheric Rivers Group_. The goal was to connect researchers investigating the atmospheric dynamics, physical processes, and climate impacts of atmospheric rivers (ARs) in Antarctica, a uniquely uninhabited and isolated region that serves as an unparalleled laboratory for studying poleward moisture transport.

Antarctic ARs are rare but intense events that carry substantial heat and moisture from the mid-latitudes to the continent. These intrusions significantly influence Antarctic climate and the surface mass balance of the ice sheet, with effects ranging from extreme snowfall to surface melt, depending on season and location. Capturing these complex processes is key to reducing uncertainty in projections of future sea-level rise.

Antarctica’s remoteness, circumpolar oceanic boundary, and absence of permanent population create ideal conditions for disentangling large-scale physical mechanisms from regional and anthropogenic noise. While the Arctic presents a different setting, continental, inhabited, and more topographically complex, many of the underlying atmospheric processes are shared. As scientific interest in the dynamics of Arctic ARs continues to grow, **AAARG!** has broadened its scope, beginning in 2025, to welcome contributions that advance understanding of AR behavior in both polar regions.

The group maintains strong ties with the [Atmospheric River Tracking Method Intercomparison Project](https://ncar.github.io/ARTMIP/intro.html) (**ARTMIP**). Several **AAARG!** members are the developers of the first algorithms specifically designed to detect atmospheric rivers in polar regions, now included among the ARDTs (Atmospheric River Detection Tools) within ARTMIP. These tools are actively used by the group to identify and characterize polar ARs, and **AAARG!** continues to contribute to the evaluation and refinement of detection methods adapted to high-latitude dynamics.

**AAARG!** brings together a diverse community of researchers across polar meteorology, atmospheric science, and climate–cryosphere interactions, working collaboratively to deepen knowledge of atmospheric rivers in high-latitude environments.
